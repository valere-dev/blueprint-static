# Copy Analysis — reading headlines, hooks and claims

How to judge the words of an ad (static headline, video hook, body line, stat, offer line), how to
classify them, how to bank them, and where the compliance traps are. For writing full video scripts the
master document is `/root/claude-vault/knowledge/copywriting/COPY-CONSTITUTION.md`; this file is the
analysis lens.

---

## 1. The seven truths (judge every line against them)

1. You sell a desire, not a product.
2. You never create desire; you channel it.
3. The market beats the copy (a starving crowd beats a brilliant ad).
4. The customer is the best copywriter (their words beat yours).
5. One ad, one idea: one sub-avatar, one angle, one promise, one mechanism, one action. Two promises
   don't add up, they compete.
6. Specificity sells; generalities are discounted. ("600 mg, 31 formulas tested, 12,400 subscribers"
   is believed; "powerful, premium, revolutionary" drags everything down.)
7. Only the numbers judge — spend, CPA, ROAS, and month-2 rebill for subscriptions.

## 2. Static headline tests (apply to every headline you bank or brief)

| Test | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| **500 ms test** (Crafter) | Answers "what is it and how does it help me?" in half a second | Needs a second read |
| **Stranger test** | Someone who never heard of the brand gets WHAT and WHAT FOR | "You can't farm this" |
| **Names the problem, not the age** | "For stubborn beer belly" | "For men over 40" |
| **Outcome, not problem** (angle rule) | "Energy back by 10 a.m." | "Lack of energy" |
| **One idea** | One promise | Two benefits stacked (dilutes) |
| **Customer words** | "dad bod", "FUPA", "bag of concrete" | "abdominal adiposity" |
| **Simplest words** | "a flat belly again" | "see your belt buckle again" |
| **No jargon** | "your fat makes estrogen" | "aromatase conversion" |
| **No soulless corporate line** | "Adieu la fatigue en 7 jours" style direct benefit | "More than a cream", "feel alive again" |
| **Congruence with the visual** | Belly text → belly image | Belly text over an arm |
| **Same text amount as template** | Same slot count | Added tagline/context/disclaimer |

80 % of a static's result is wording (Crafter). The best wording comes from review mining and golden
nuggets, not from the copywriter.

## 3. Headline pattern library

### 3.1 Universal patterns (H1–H16, from the blueprint skill)
| Category | Patterns |
|---|---|
| A Offer / urgency | H1 [offer hook] · H2 LAST CHANCE — SALE ENDS IN [X] HOURS · H3 SAVE $[amount] ON YOUR [transformation] |
| B Differentiation | H4 THE ORIGINAL VS THE COPYCATS · H5 [OLD SOLUTION] IS OVER. |
| C Desire / emotion | H6 THEY WON'T SAY IT. BUT THEY'LL FEEL IT. · H7 I'M [best result] AT [age/timeframe] · H8 [TIMEFRAME] TO [desired result] |
| D Transformation | H9 ONE [PRODUCT]. [N] PROBLEMS SOLVED. · H10 Struggling with [main pain]? · H11 [SPECIFIC METRIC] OF [PROBLEM] GONE |
| E Pain | H12 [X YEARS] OF [PAIN] GONE IN [TIMEFRAME] |
| F Humour / meme | H13 [SPECIFIC CONTEXT]. [UNEXPECTED OUTCOME]. · H14 [THIRD PARTY] FOUND [AVATAR]'S SECRET. · H15 [THIRD PARTY] BEGS FOR [MORE] · H16 A [MEASURABLE VISIBLE PROOF] EVERY [TIMEFRAME]. |

### 3.2 Observed winning headline families in supplements (verbatim examples, testosterone market)
| Family | Examples (source) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Reframe: "X is not Y, it's Z"** | "It Was Never Fat. It Was Estrogen." · "PUFFY NIPPLES? THAT'S NOT CHEST FAT. IT'S A HORMONE SIGNAL." · "A stubborn gut isn't a discipline problem." · "The Belly That Won't Budge Is NOT Fat. It's Cortisol." (Primal Edge) | Gives permission ("not your fault") + plants the mechanism in one line |
| **Time contrast** | "12 YEARS OF THIS?? GONE IN 21 DAYS" (Primal Edge, top static, 7 variants) · "13 years of beer belly / man boobs gone in 9 days" (Primal Viking video) | Long suffering vs short fix; Crafter's "7 years of cellulite gone in 6 weeks" pattern |
| **Guarantee AS headline** | "+1 INCH IN 30 DAYS OR YOUR MONEY BACK" (Primal Viking) · "+3 INCHES OR MONEY BACK", "SHE'LL FORGET HER VIBRATOR OR MONEY BACK" (Primal Storm) · "THE 90-DAY CHALLENGE. MELT 27 LBS OR MONEY BACK." (Primal Viking, top share) | Promise + risk reversal in one; bold |
| **Tested-N comparison** | "3 ORGAN SUPPLEMENTS. 1 BEER BELLY. WHICH ONE ACTUALLY WORKS?" (Elora, Primal Edge) · "I Tested 3 Organ Supplements For My Beer Belly. Here's The Truth." (Primal Edge's most duplicated ad: 13 copies) | Curiosity + category attack + implied proof |
| **Us vs them / source** | "REINDEER VS BISON" · "WHICH ANIMAL'S ORGANS WOULD YOU TRUST?" · "COUNT THE ANIMALS." · "2,000 LB WILD vs 350 LB FARMED" | Attack the category (or a farmed source), never name-and-shame wrongly |
| **Anti-waste / receipt** | "I SPENT $1,750 ON SUPPLEMENTS. MY GUT NEVER MOVED." · "Wildhorn vs 12 Pills" · "It's not a supplement problem. It's a FOOD problem." | Price anchored to past waste, not to a cheaper rival |
| **Authenticity / fakes** | "BEWARE OF FAKES" · "WATCH OUT FOR SCAMS." · "$75 GONE. DON'T DO WHAT I DID." · "THE FRONT IS MARKETING. THE BACK IS TRUTH." (Elora) | Turns copycat competition into a reason to buy direct |
| **Villain as character** | "WANTED FOR CRIMES AGAINST AMERICAN MEN" (Elora's top ad, 11.6 % est. share) · "ESTROGEN WAS FOUND DEAD." | The enemy (mechanism) becomes a person; pattern interrupt |
| **Callout question** | "DOES YOUR CHEST STILL LOOK LIKE THIS?" · "You Haven't Taken Your Shirt Off in Front of Anyone in How Long?" | Selection in the first words |
| **Third-party witness** | "She'll Feel the Difference. So Will You." · "My wife can't stop saying how different I am" · "His wife can't keep her hands off him" | Proof = her sentence; desire shown, not said |
| **Generation / enemy outside** | "Your grandfather had more T at 60" (Mars Men) · "Don't take T. Make T." | External culprit; anti-TRT identity |
| **Offer pretext** | "WE MADE TOO MUCH" (Mars Men, 493 days live — the oldest winner of 4 brands) · "50% OFF FOR LIFE" · "OFFICIAL APOLOGY STATEMENT" · "THIS DEAL IS INSANE" (Ancestral) | Reason-why for the discount |
| **Timeline** | "WEEK 0 / WEEK 2 / WEEK 4" · "9 Days to Feel It. 30 Days to See It. 90 Days to Transform." | Staged delays raise likelihood |

### 3.3 Time-contrast amplifiers (Crafter static #8)
Amplify the pain with one of: time · effort · money spent · consequences if nothing changes · comparison
with intense efforts · repetition of the problem · a quantified visual measure · felt injustice. Then
step week by week using the exact SECONDARY symptoms ("heavy, swollen legs in week 2"), never generic
claims.

## 4. Video hooks

### 4.1 The triple test (one failure = throw it away)
1. Does it name the audience? 2. Does it imply a benefit? 3. Does it open a curiosity that isn't closed?

### 4.2 Nineteen hook types (Crafter, adopted in the Constitution)
1 Direct benefit · 2 Humour · 3 Audience call-out · 4 Direct objection ("Can this really…?") · 5 Answer
an objection ("…so we tested it") · 6 Pain point · 7 Question (ideally one that recurs in your
comments) · 8 Big claim (about the mechanism/input, never a body-result number) · 9 Fast claim · 10
Authority · 11 Before/after (verbal, or of an object) · 12 Comparison with the category · 13 Destroy a
limiting belief · 14 Information · 15 Novelty · 16 Exclusivity · 17 Belief reversal ("I thought it was a
scam until…") · 18 Solution call-out ("don't buy another X before…") · 19 Call-out + curiosity, with a
physical scroll-stop.

Six classic leads by awareness: Offer (most aware) · Promise (product-aware) · Problem–solution
(problem-aware) · Secret (named hidden mechanism) · Proclamation · Story (unaware). One lead per script.

### 4.3 Hook anatomy
- Second 1 = capture (movement, rupture). Second 2 = selection (the sub-avatar and their situation).
  Second 3+ = sobriety.
- Three layers vary separately: image (first frame/motion), on-screen text, voice. The feed starts
  muted: the first word READ isn't the first word SAID. Sound-off test.
- Intensity is its own variable: factual → dramatised → shock. The best is rarely the strongest.
- The hook is targeting: a problem-specific hook stops fewer, better people. Judge on spend/CPA.
- A hook must meet an existing state ("she already dreads bedtime"); if you can't write that state,
  you're educating — and paying for competitors.

### 4.4 Verbatim hooks worth studying (bison market)
- "If you think two capsules can't flatten a beer belly you've had for 10 years, watch what happens
  inside your gut over the next 90 days."
- "If your gut feels like a bag of concrete you carry around all day…"
- "Your wife says snack less. Your doctor says it's just your age. They're both partially right."
- "If you're swallowing 12 supplements every morning and your gut still hasn't moved, the answer isn't
  another bottle."
- "The cheaper organ supplement is the better one. That shouldn't be possible."
- "These two bottles look almost identical," (Primal Edge, authenticity UGC)
- "I quit TRT, started Mars Men. Got leaner than I ever did on injections." (Mars Men, 149 days, 45
  variants)
Pattern: condition callout ("If you…") + a felt metaphor or a contradiction + an open loop.

## 5. Claims, stats and proof lines

- **Stats are benefit claims phrased like the template's stat**, with the survey footnote:
  "92 % reported clearer minds" → "93 % saw their beer belly shrink*" (*12-week customer survey).
  Use only stats the product sheet allows.
- **Specificity moves from the result to the input** (Constitution): "600 mg per capsule", "31 formulas
  tested", "12,400 active subscribers", "$1.60 a day" — believed and compliant. Body-result numbers
  ("lose 12 kg", "+40 % testosterone") are the risky kind.
- **Numbers in a source ad are never copied** as our facts. Replace with our inputs or remove.
- Proof placement: right after the mechanism, where the viewer asks for it.
- **Deadly sincerity**: a real flaw before the benefits ("It takes three weeks, not three days") raises
  belief in everything else.
- **Duration is the mechanism**: staged benefits (day 7 / 30 / 90); the subscription explained as a
  protocol phase.
- **Who notices** (identity): the wife, the colleague, the kids — derived from real reviews.
- Weird credible numbers that worked for competitors: 1979, 40 years of research, 400,000 years,
  2,000 lb vs 350 lb, $1,750, "14 carrots / 24 oysters / 12 Brazil nuts / 9 ribeyes or 2 capsules".
  Credible because specific — but each must be TRUE for our brand before use.

## 6. Offer lines and CTA

- One CTA, one action; say what happens after the click.
- One booster at most (time, stock, scarcity, social proof, guarantee, bonus) — only if real. On
  subscriptions, no fake scarcity (recruits deal-hunters who churn at the second charge).
- The guarantee is the climax, restated against the angle: "Flat gut or your money back", "Chest back or
  every penny back", "She'll notice or you won't pay".
- Reason-why for any discount (production surplus, packaging change, new market test). Naked discounts
  aren't believed.
- Change the price reference: the $120 consultation, the four dead bottles, the daily coffee, $/day —
  never another supplement.
- Statics: never mention autoship/subscription mechanics; use "50 % off for life" style framing.

## 7. Compliance — the two-level policy

**Level 1 — Landing pages / website** (Stripe/Shopify-safe): structure-function language only
("formulated to support healthy testosterone*" + FDA disclaimer). Never cure/treat/block as hard
claims on-site. Ad-level claims are not carried to the landing page without a landing-page check.

**Level 2 — Ads**: aggressive is allowed and expected (the founder will say if it isn't aggressive
enough), but the following stay off-limits in OUR ads even when competitors use them:

| ❌ Forbidden | ✅ Rewrite as |
|---|---|
| 2nd person asserting a health state ("Do you suffer from low T?", "Your belly…") in video scripts | 1st/3rd person ("Most men after 40 notice…", "I was 41 and…"); 2nd person OK for situations/behaviours |
| Cure / treat / prevent / heal / drug / disease names | "Supports", "helps maintain", mechanism description |
| White coats, "9 out of 10 doctors", fake doctor or founder | Real users, real third-party tests, mechanism as documented |
| Fake news article, real media logo, invented by-line, fake date | Brand-owned content, brand as source |
| Fake platform UI (fake play buttons, fake notifications/comments) | Native formats that don't imitate the platform's UI |
| Denigrating a named brand | Attack the category ("farmed reindeer", "the most popular beef-organ supplements") |
| Invented testimonials; atypical results as typical; fake before/after bodies presented as real | Real customers; illustrated/symbolic before-after clearly not a real person; object/life-scene before/after |
| Free trial + auto-renewal without disclosure | Renewal price + frequency + how to cancel |
| Celebrity deepfakes, invented studies, invented numbers | Nothing. If it isn't in the product sheet, it doesn't exist |
| Diagnosis claims in quizzes ("your testosterone is suppressed", "your body functions like a 79-year-old") | "Signs of…" |

Observed in competitors and NOT to copy: fake media partnerships (NY Post / Thrillist co-branded
listicles, Mars Men), "doctors profit from your symptoms" conspiracy, celebrity endorsements we don't
have, "+60 % T" result numbers, unverifiable "results in 24 hours".

When analyzing a competitor line, always add the **compliance flag** column: OK / risky (why) / banned
(why) + the nearest compliant rewrite with the same function. Compliance is a slot filler, not a final
check: in the transplant study it was the most frequent change (36 hits, 10/10 ads).

## 8. Copy diagnosis grid (use on any ad that "doesn't work")

| Symptom in the copy | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Two promises | Angle mixing | One script = one angle |
| Abstract headline | Fails stranger test | Name the thing + what it does |
| Age callout | Demographic, not problem | Name the problem |
| Features list | Product-centric | Result vs what they tried |
| Jargon | Writer-centric | 14-year-old pass |
| Generic praise proof | Dust, not nugget | Golden nugget or proof object |
| Off-angle bubble (hangover, brain…) | Drift | Name the core problem in every line |
| Long hook | Hook not targeting | 5–8 spoken words, one idea |
| Hook ≠ body | Random hook | Congruent hook from the body |
| "Better than [brand]" | Named denigration | Attack the category |
| No reason-why on the discount | Naked discount | Add the true cause |

## 9. Which AI to use for copy (study, 30 Sep 2026)

Study: ~8,200 X + Reddit posts, 820 relevant, 344 from credible pros (files:
`/root/workspace/copy-ai-study/quotes.md`, `workflows.md`, `stats.json`).

Findings:
- **Usage**: Claude dominates (~5:1 among pros) for hooks, advertorials, emails, VSLs — because of
  Projects/Skills and review mining.
- **Raw text quality**: since July, many top operators rate GPT-5.6 Sol then GPT-6 Astra (Sept) above
  Claude for tight direct-response copy (Stefan Georgi: Astra "probably the best out of any frontier
  model"; Dan Shipper: Astra main writer, Sonnet 5.5 better at revision; Lorenzo Pravata ranks Sol above
  Fable for native ad copy). Complaint about Claude: too cautious / wordy / "Ted Talk" for aggressive DR.
  Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 (late Sept) regain some praise; little data yet.
- **How pros actually work**: research first (cluster 100–500 reviews into pains/desires/objections,
  pull exact phrases) → one Project per product (brand doc, ICP research, winners, top VSL transcript) →
  skills for the process (hook skill: extract the winning angle, write 9 hooks across relevance /
  curiosity / emotion, cut the weak) → an anti-slop rules file (banned words, rhythm, own voice samples)
  → blind A/B of models on the same brief.
- **Skeptics**: raw output is "AI slop" without research and winners; audiences now spot ChatGPT-style
  AND Claude-style scripts.

House recommendation: **Claude for research, decoding and structure** (this skill); test Astra vs
Opus/Sonnet 5.5 on the same brief for the final copy lines; always feed the model the brand's winners
and verbatim customer language. Plain chat writes better prose than coding-agent system prompts.
