# Frameworks: Evolve × Crafter, distilled

Two courses are the reference base for every creative framework in this system:
- **Evolve** (Spencer / EZ Media, with Shaun & Carlo) — the master reference. Synthesis on the VPS:
  `/srv/mrr/evolve.html`. When in doubt about structure, angles, variations or iterations: "base it on
  Evolve".
- **Crafter Club** (Nick / Creative Crafters, French course) — the execution and iteration toolkit.
  Extractions: `/root/workspace/adn-crafter/strategy.md`, `creative.md`, `execution.md`,
  `missing-lessons.md`; transcripts `/root/workspace/crafter/txt_api/` (4 lessons were badly
  downloaded and are missing: creative structure, quick wins, TrendTrack research 1 and 2).

Both agree on the core: **sell a desire, not a product; niche the message, not the product; answer the
market's sophistication; change one thing at a time; micro-details are not tests.** Crafter credits
Evolve for the sub-desire idea.

## 1. Evolve — the Avatar OS

The chain: **PRODUCT → DESIRES → MARKET SOPHISTICATION → CORE AVATAR → SUB-AVATARS → ANGLES → ADS.**

### 1.1 Product (step 0)
- Every product is 2 products: physical (what it is) and functional (what it does). People buy what it
  does.
- Mechanism = HOW the product delivers the result, not what it does ("supplement that makes you lose
  weight" → "peptide 47D blocks sugar absorption, so the body burns fat").
- Product Overview Document before any test: name + price · how it works (6th grade) · 5+ features ·
  assumed benefits · assumed desires · assumed new mechanisms (USP) · hidden mechanisms.
- Error #1: targeting desires the product can't honour.

### 1.2 Desires (Schwartz)
- You can't create desire, only channel it.
- 6 core desires (2026 power ranking): Health/Survival · Status · Sex/Relationships · Comfort · Control ·
  Belonging. Plus mass technological problems (complexity, overwhelm, fragility, maintenance…).
- Surface desires ("I want better sleep") are what avatars are built on; always know the core desire
  feeding them.
- Evaluate a desire on scope (how many), urgency (now?), staying power (recurring vs one-shot).
- Elevation strategy: move the product up the desire chain to open less saturated markets (stay
  authentic).
- Desire calendar: Sept structure/fresh start · Oct identity · Nov gratitude + BF · Dec connection · Jan
  health/control/status (+35–40 % supplement spend) · Feb Valentine's · Mar–Apr renewal · May–Jun
  achievement · Jul–Aug back-to-school. If performance drops, check where seasonal desires went.

### 1.3 Market sophistication (5 stages)
| Stage | Situation | Hook that works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First to market | Pure claim |
| 2 | Competition emerges | Benefits, "mine is better" |
| 3 | Skeptical market | NEW MECHANISM → new hope |
| 4 | Competing mechanisms | Mechanism amplified (faster, stronger) |
| 5 | Saturation | NEW IDENTITY |
99 % of e-com is in stage 3–5. Three responses: **new mechanism** (most powerful; communicate simply),
**new information** (most accessible: educate on what they don't know → authority → trust; never invent,
never fear-monger; "would you run this ad to your mom?"), **new identity** (hardest; skip for 99 %).

### 1.4 Avatars
- Goal = RELATABILITY: "That's me" → trust → buy. "Fuck demographics."
- 5 categories, strongest first: desires · experiences · emotions · behaviours/habits · demographics
  (last).
- Core avatar = one category (one desire). Sub-avatar = core + 1–3 layered categories.
- One desire per avatar (two = soft avatar). The broader the avatar, the better the product/offer/skill
  must be.
- Revenue stage → number of core avatars: 0–100k/month: 1 (depth in sub-avatars, pick a desire a
  competitor scales, 2–4 weeks min) · 100–500k: 1, execution · 500k–1M: 2–3 · 1–10M: 3–10 + mechanism
  + info · 10M+: volume monster.

### 1.5 Desire hunting (research)
Sources in order: Reddit (most honest focus group) · YouTube videos + comments · TikTok comments ·
Amazon reviews (yours + competitors) · post-purchase survey · AnswerThePublic.
Sheet with 3 columns: Desire (I want X) · Customer language (verbatim, never paraphrased) · Ad ideas.
"Your research works more on you than you work on it." Never delegate 100 % to AI; always fact-check.

### 1.6 Creative strategy vocabulary
- **Concept (swing)**: the big idea tested, centred on YOU ("test UGC", "test us-vs-them", "iterate a
  winner").
- **Angle**: the reason to buy, centred on the CUSTOMER. "Us-vs-them of WHAT?" — the WHAT is the angle.
  Every ad needs one; always write it down.
- **Variation**: a different way to communicate the angle.
- **Format**: how the ad is delivered.
Why an ad flops: no emotional impact · no curiosity · no payoff · too complex (aim grade 5–6) · empty,
bossy or repetitive claims · written not spoken · written for you not the avatar · angle too broad ·
wrong format. The easier the format (image), the better the messaging must be.

### 1.7 Copy rules
Customer lens only · write like people talk · slippery slide · research = 80 % (2–3 h per hour of
writing) · value equation · Kyle's frames (new & only, easy & anybody, safe & predictable, big & fast) ·
create your own category · show, don't tell · body copy = objection → claim → proof → benefit ·
awareness levels: never skip steps.

### 1.8 Testing — the three "guns"
| Method | Fixed | Moves | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marksman** | 1 concept | 3 variations = 3 different angles | Find a direction fast; images; initial tests; specific hooks, generic hold |
| **Sniper** | 1 angle | 3 hooks/executions of the same angle | Dig for the super-winner; videos; follow-ups; specific hold |
| **Shotgun** | nothing | everything | Raw UGC seeding only — never creative strategy |
Workflow: Marksman → Sniper → Marksman again at plateau. Volume = manpower: ~1 concept/day, 15–20 ads/week.
Feedback loop: identify the sub-avatar/angle/desire behind a winner — never "it's a video so it works".

### 1.9 Iterating a winner — the 6-axis matrix
1. Length (15/30/45 s) · 2. Gender · 3. Filming location ("where else would people use this?") ·
4. Actor age · 5. Ethnicity · 6. New ways to say the same thing (filmed as real new creatives).
Principle: change the execution, keep the angle + winning structure.

### 1.10 Hooks & scripts
Hook = first 3 s: call out the right audience + promise a benefit + make them want more. Higher in the
funnel (unaware) = more scalable. Script = **3 hooks + 3 bridges (~8 s) + 1 universal hold**
(objection / claim / proof / benefit + CTA), ~43–45 s. Draft → reinforce → refine (read aloud) →
visuals → reflect. Always validate claims.

### 1.11 13 winning static templates (Evolve's Canva set)
Avatar callout · solution exaggeration · stealing credibility · specific pain · accidental ideal outcome
· relatable hook · indirect avatar · free/offer · simplified us-vs-them (Nutella) · puberty style ·
minimal us-vs-them.

### 1.12 Media buying context (for judging "winners")
One CBO "arena" campaign; if Meta spends on an ad, it's a winner 9 times out of 10; spend IS signal #1.
3-2-2 per ad set (3 creatives, 2 copies, 2 headlines; one concept per ad set). Test 3–7 days, never
kill before 72 h. Verdicts: loser · KPI winner · spend winner · breakthrough · super winner. Soft
metrics (hook ≥ 25–30 %, hold ≈ 8 %, CTR ≥ 1 %) point to levers; only ROAS/CPA decide. Ad name/batch
number on every ad set and ad. Growth Guide: ad type = iteration (mine) / imitation (others') /
ideation (my research).

### 1.13 Terminal errors
Purely demographic avatar · attacking a stage-5 tier-1 market with a mechanism hook · testing one desire
for 5 days then pivoting (min 2–4 weeks + 5 sub-avatars) · two desires in one avatar.

## 2. Crafter — the execution toolkit

### 2.1 Primary desires (13, after Reiss)
Acceptance · Curiosity (pattern breaker: forbid what everyone does) · Family · Idealism (prove it) ·
Independence · Order · Physical activity (guilt) · Power/money (Nick's favourite) · Romance · Saving
(express as an object: "save a Rolex a year") · Social contact · Tranquility · Revenge (a person, or the
industry's common enemy).
Every creative: **1. name the desire, 2. make it felt (show it, exaggerate), 3. offer a credible
mechanism.**

### 2.2 Sub-desire and exclusion
Narrower desire = narrower audience. "Be a big fish in a small pond." Niche creatives, funnel and LP
together; the product can stay mass-market. Build MOFU/BOFU creatives on the same niche desire.
4 exclusion levers: who / what / when (trigger) / why (cause + motivation). Niching resets
sophistication, raises relevance, lowers the cost of attention. "I've almost never seen a creative too
targeted."

### 2.3 Sophistication ladder (5 rungs) — play N or N+1, never N−1
1 claim · 2 bigger claim · 3 new mechanism · 4 better mechanism · 5 identity/community. Diagnose from
competitors' top-10 scaling creatives (simple claims = low; long mechanism VSLs = 3–4; pure branding = 5).
Sophistication is per audience; exclusion is the cheapest reset. Never over-sophisticate a virgin market.

### 2.4 Mechanism = Freshness × Belief; UMP + UMS
Three differentiation cases: format transplant (easiest) · desire transplant · core swap. Inspiration
by case: same desire → copy how they market the desire; same mechanism → copy how they explain it;
same desire + mechanism, other format → say why yours is better, use their negative reviews.
"You don't create belief, you borrow it." Go with existing beliefs, dismantle wrong ones one by one.

### 2.5 Positioning
The prospect stands in a supermarket aisle full of the same promises. UMP: why the problem happens AND
why everything they tried failed, in detail. UMS: "A + B = C". Simplify for a 14-year-old. Map
competitors on the same desire (TrendTrack "similar shops"), study their top 4–5 winners, pick the
edge (mechanism, authority, narrower sub-avatar). Think whole funnel. "E-commerce is hope."

### 2.6 Authority (7 figures) — authority validates the MECHANISM, not the product
Country/culture · celebrity · expert · the one who embodies the result · institution · peer · **the
desired one** (the person the buyer wants to please: a pretty woman saying "the best T-shirt for men";
a daughter buying for her dad). Coherence: a wrong authority figure destroys the whole argument. No
deepfakes, no invented studies.

### 2.7 Data: internal vs external; golden nuggets
Internal (ad comments, BM stats, support tickets, objections, reviews, post-purchase surveys) = higher
hit rate. External (competitors, trends, persona conversations, out-of-niche brands) = big swings.
Golden nugget = a review/comment that nails you to the wall: validated by many, the target recognises
itself totally, an instant click. Wording sits under the angle (angle "grow your hair"; wording
"receding temples"). When a new wording wins, re-run all past winners with it ("cascade").
"Your customer is your best copywriter." "The customer must feel we stole their diary."

### 2.8 The creative equation
**Creative = Format + Angle (the outcome, not the problem) + Persona + Product + Hook (+ funnel stage)**,
written into the ad name. Sort BM by spend, decompose the top creatives, change one variable at a time,
keep a table. Never iterate micro-details. A headline change that keeps the angle is a valid iteration.

### 2.9 Value equation (Hormozi applied)
Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) / (Time delay × Effort). Show every component on screen.
Don't cut delay/effort so far that likelihood collapses. Likelihood raisers: authority, debunking what
fails, staged timelines, guarantee, native well-acted execution, visible mechanism, hyper-specific
persona, naming the medication they already take.

### 2.10 Script blocks, hooks, CTA
Base: Hook → Problem → False solution → Product intro → Features → CTA. Classic brief: Hook → restate
problem → solution → product in use → social proof (always, many faces) → angle-specific objections →
CTA. Structure depends on awareness. One script = one angle. More promises = less credibility. Zero
repetition. Hook = targeting; don't optimise hook rate. 19 hook types (see copy-analysis.md). One CTA +
one booster. 4 B-rolls always requested.

### 2.11 Formats and statics
19+ formats (IG-story testimonial, UGC, podcast, news set, comparison, mash-up, unboxing, comment
reply, green screen, street interview, founder, tutorial, "3 reasons", POV, interview, 2–3 person
discussion, ASMR, silent aesthetic, storytelling). Every video format can become a static and vice
versa. "If it doesn't work, it's your angle and your message, not your format."
Statics: 80 % of the result is wording; 500 ms test; simplicity scales; never soulless corporate lines;
explicit direct benefits; congruence; visual hierarchy ("close your eyes, open them"); a static has a
hook. 10 static templates + 11 winning statics decomposed (see copy-analysis.md and template-reading.md).

### 2.12 Iteration framework
Winner = ROAS > break-even; beginner ≥ €500 spend profitable; agency ≥ 10–15 % of monthly spend
(20–25 % = very big). Process: find winners (sort by spend) → decompose (equation) → cross with other
proven angles/personas/formats (find the gaps) → produce fast. Variables: angles · formats · actor age ·
ethnicity · gender (education for women often works better with a male presenter) · location · edit ·
length (shorter = lower funnel) · other product · hook/headline · persona · funnel stage · language/market
· static ↔ video · answer a top comment · isolate a sub-angle. Wording rule: when transposing a
winner, keep the same hook/headline ~80 % of the time.

### 2.13 Scale ("we don't scale budgets, we scale creatives")
6 levers + 1 multiplier: angles · personas/sub-avatars · diversity · iterations · full funnel + congruence
(one LP per avatar/angle at scale) · volume; multiplier = a tight data feedback loop. Funnel spend base
60 % ToF/MoF, 25 % awareness 3–4, 15 % most aware; never starve the top. 70–80 % of tests fail. 20–30
creatives before killing a hypothesis. Unaware winners have ~20× the impact.

## 3. Where they disagree — and the house rule

| Topic | Evolve | Crafter | House rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who is the avatar | Experiences + emotions + behaviours; demographics = terminal error | Exclusion by who/what/when/why; "who" = sex, age, ethnicity | Sub-avatar = when (trigger) + why (cause) + what they tried. Demographics only as a filter, on screen via the actor |
| Angle change | A new angle = a new test (Marksman) | Angle is one variable you iterate | A new angle = NEW (it changes who the ad reaches) |
| Rewording a winner | Axis 6: same message, new words | Keep the exact hook ~80 % when moving format | Changing the visual → keep the words. Iterating the script on the same visual → reword. Never both |
| Script shape | 3 hooks + 3 bridges + 1 hold, ~45 s | A chain of blocks, no timings; 1–2 angle objections; social proof always | Evolve's modules and timings filled with Crafter's blocks |
| Hook rate | Soft metric; ROAS/CPA decide | Hook = targeting; a specific hook stops fewer, better people | Judge hooks on spend and CPA, never on hook rate alone |
| Test volume | 3-2-2 per ad set | 20–30 creatives before killing a hypothesis; 7–8+ for an angle-persona fit | 3-2-2 is the ad-set shell; a hypothesis dies only after 20–30 creatives across ad sets |
| Sophistication answers | New mechanism, new information, identity | 5 rungs; niching (exclusion) resets the level | Crafter's 5 rungs, N or N+1, exclusion as the cheapest reset; Evolve's "new information" kept as an option |
| Scripted vs freestyle | Write how people talk | Freestyle/"ugly" ads = biggest winners | Scripts must sound freestyle: customer phrasing, hesitations, real reactions; nothing polished |
| Concept vs iteration | Concept = idea tested; variation = new way to say the angle | No formal concept term; "wording" ≈ variation | Use Evolve's terms; Crafter's equation for naming |
| Format meaning | Ad delivery | Also product delivery format (gummy vs powder) | Two different things: ad format vs product format |

## 4. How they combine — the merged AI-assisted pipeline

| Step | What | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Product & mechanism | Features → benefits → UMP (why what they tried failed) → UMS ("A + B = C"); freshness × belief; beat competitors' top 5 ads | 1 product sheet |
| 2 Desires & wording | Primary desire, surface desires with verbatim quotes and golden nuggets (Reddit, TikTok, reviews, ad comments, tickets) | 10–30 desires + wording bank |
| 3 Sophistication | Read competitors' top-10 scaling ads → level 1–5 → response N or N+1 | 1 level + 1 response |
| 4 Sub-avatars | Exclusion (trigger + cause + failed solutions), 7 questions, volume check | 3–10 per core desire |
| 5 Angles + authority | Per sub-avatar: the outcome in their words + the authority figure validating the mechanism | 1–3 angles each |
| 6 Pre-brief + script | Inspiration link, sub-avatar, UMS, desire, angle, hypothesis; 3 hooks + 3 bridges + 1 hold | 3 ads per script |
| 7 Visual brief | Format, actor, location, length, edit + the 4 b-rolls | 1 editor brief |
| 8 Learnings loop | Winners decomposed (visual, audio, hook, persona); 4 tables: persona, winners, losers, iterations (spot gaps); weekly recap | back to 4, 5 or the moves |

TL;DR (Evolve): Product (mechanism) + Desire (surface) + Sub-avatar (1–3 layered categories) + Angle
(hook entry point) + Sophistication response = winning ad. The only output that counts: "That's me" →
trust → buy.

## 5. Using the frameworks in ANALYSIS (not production)

When decoding a competitor ad, run these lenses in order and write one line each:
1. **Desire** named / shown / mechanism offered? (Crafter's 3 steps) Which of the 13?
2. **Sophistication response** used (claim / bigger claim / new mechanism / better mechanism /
   identity / new information / exclusion)?
3. **Awareness** where the ad starts and when the product appears (unaware reveals late ~12 s;
   problem-aware 8–10 s; product-aware opens on the product).
4. **Sub-avatar** by exclusion (who/what/when/why/tried).
5. **Angle** as outcome (one sentence).
6. **Mechanism** UMP/UMS + authority figure type.
7. **Value equation**: which of the 4 components is on screen; which is missing.
8. **Creative equation**: format + angle + persona + product + hook + funnel stage → the code you'd name
   it with.
9. **Diagnosis**: what lever they are iterating across their variants (that's what they believe in).
10. **Gap**: the combination (winning format × winning angle) they have NOT made — our first test.

Note on ratios: older notes recommend a fixed 80/20 (iterations/new concepts) or 60/40. The current
house method is to clone proven winners and not impose a fixed ratio; use Crafter's flex (hot period →
more iterations; nothing working → more net-new) only as a sanity check.
