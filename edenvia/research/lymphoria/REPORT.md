Status: **draft for the founder's sign-off (gate 1: angles + avatar)**. Date 2026-10-11. Sources: the 35 statics decoded in
`1A-ads.md` (Brandsearch, EU-spend ranked), Trendtrack and Brandsearch brand data (1B below), the founder's A8 avatar sheet
(`edenvia/avatars/lymphatic-bloating.md`). Every line points to a source ad (#n) or a tool. Nothing invented.

less puffy, legs and belly that finally respond. We clone the buyer, the structures and the proof devices. We never clone
the product claims (herbs, "drain", disease names).

## 10. Their weaknesses (sourced)

- **Disease and condition naming in copy**: PCOS (#11 text), menopause (#19, 26, 29), "lip edema" (their video), "liver failure" (Trendtrack #Dr. Micheal Jones ads). FTC/Meta exposure; Edenvia will not copy these words (brand.json bans them).
- **Inconsistent proof**: 30 / 60 / 90-day guarantee; "90,000+ customers" vs "200+ doctors"; a "WEEK 4 · WEEK 4" typo on #20.
- **Creative sameness**: one 2×2 timeline template cloned ten times; the audience sees the same layout for every body part.
- **Page churn**: Brandsearch shows 0 active ads on the main lymphoria.co page and the spend moving through persona pages ("Ancient Roots" 4,542 ads / 227 k€ EU spend; "Herbalist Amara Linden, PhD"; "Lymphologist Allison James, PhD"); Trendtrack shows 10,382 statics across pages such as "Herbal Remedies" (~3,800 active), "Lymphatic Wellness Explained", "Dr. Micheal Jones". Fake-expert pages are a bannable pattern.
- **Scale**: 5.4 M monthly visits (Brandsearch) and ~230 k€ measured EU spend on one page alone: Edenvia will not out-spend them; it has to out-specify (the molecule, the glass of water, 8 PPM) inside their proven structures.
